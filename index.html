          His glucose
          Import a file from his CGM, pump or meter (CSV, Excel, PDF, JSON or zip) to see his graphs here, with meals and events on top.
          
        
          
          
          
        
          
      
          
      
          
      
          
      
          
        How to get the file
        
            
          
          • Omnipod 5 / Glooko: sign in at my.glooko.com on a computer and choose Export to CSV. You get a .zip file. Import the .zip as it is.
            • 
          
          • Dexcom: sign in at clarity.dexcom.eu, click the export icon (top right), pick the dates and export. Import the .csv.
            • 
          
          • Medtronic (MiniMed 780G, Guardian sensor): sign in at carelink.minimed.eu on a computer, open Reports, choose Data export (CSV), pick the dates and export. Import the .csv.
            • 
          
          • Libre: sign in at libreview.com, open Glucose History and choose Download glucose data. Import the .csv.
            • 
          
          • Any other app, meter or spreadsheet: export or save it as CSV, Excel (.xlsx) or JSON. If I don't recognise the columns, you'll be asked to pick the date and glucose columns.
            • 
          
          • PDF reports: import them too. Logbook or daily-detail PDFs that list readings with times work best. You'll see the readings to check before anything is saved. Summary PDFs that only show graphs and averages have no readings in them to import.
            • 
          
          • Import every week or two. New files merge with what's already here, so overlapping dates are fine.

            Sick-day rules (general UK guidance)
            
      
            Your diabetes team's written sick-day plan comes first. Use this page as a reminder, not instead of it.
            
      
              
        
            • Never stop insulin. He still needs basal insulin even if he isn't eating.
              • 
        
            • Check glucose (CGM plus a finger prick if it doesn't match how he seems) and blood ketones every 2 hours while unwell or when glucose stays high.
              • 
        
            • Pump: high glucose with ketones of 0.6 or more can mean the cannula or pod has failed. Follow your team's plan: usually a correction by pen, then a new site.
              • 
        
            • Keep him drinking small sips often. Sugar-free fluids if glucose is high, sugary ones if it's low and he won't eat.
              • 
        
            • Phone the diabetes team early. Young children become dehydrated and unwell faster than older children.
              • 
      
            
      
            Go to A&E or call 999 if
        ketones are 3.0 or more · he is vomiting and ketones are raised · he is drowsy, confused, breathing fast or deeply, or has tummy pain · you can't reach the team and you are worried.

            Backup and restore
            Save everything (log, food, glucose data, growth, check-ups, supplies and care plan) as one file on your phone or computer. Keep a copy somewhere safe, like email or Google Drive.
            
      
            
        
            Last backup—Never
            
        
            Saved items—Entries, days of CGM, records
            
      
            
      
            
      
            
      
            
    
            
    
            How restore works
            • Download backup saves one file with everything: log, food, glucose data, growth, check-ups, supplies and care plan.
            • Restore from backup shows what's in the file and asks before changing anything. It adds back anything missing and resets matching items to the backup. It never deletes entries you've added since.
            • Use it to move from the test HTML file to the family app, to a new phone, or to recover after a mistake.
